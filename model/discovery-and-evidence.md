# From a strategic bet to evidence

*Serves: BELIEFS 3.2 (outcomes), 3.4 (four product risks), 3.5 (learning loops), 4.2 (falsifiable bets), and 5.5 (tests that teach the wrong lesson).*

An initiative is a **hypothesis about how to move an outcome**. Delivery can be part of a test, but shipping alone does not validate the hypothesis. This is a lightweight guide, not a required discovery stage gate or another reporting ritual.

## Before investing substantially

1. **State the job and the bet.** Whose completed task or business result should change? Which strategy and term objective does it serve?
2. **Identify the most dangerous unknown.** Is it **value** (will anybody want the change?), **usability** (can they use it?), **feasibility** (can we deliver it?) or **viability** (does it work economically, legally and operationally)? The biggest risk may change over time.
3. **Choose the cheapest credible learning method.** Examples include observing real work, customer interviews, existing funnel analysis, a prototype, a technical spike, a service simulation or a limited pilot. Match the effort to reversibility and consequence; don't prescribe an elaborate experiment for a low-stakes change.
4. **Agree what would change your mind before seeing results.** What result supports the bet, weakens it or leaves it unresolved? Name measurement limitations, relevant segments and guardrails. Correlation or a convenient proxy is not automatically causal proof.
5. **Make an explicit next decision.** Continue, change, stop or gather better evidence. Record who made that decision, what evidence it used and what remains uncertain.

## Put the learning where it will be used

- **At gembas:** say what was tested, what was observed (with evidence grade), and which hypothesis changes next. Keep a link in `gembas/<date>-<team>.md`; no new experiment database is mandatory.
- **In check-ins:** connect observed movement or non-movement to the KR, and say when the metric is only a proxy. Check for guardrail harm or unintended effects.
- **At term review:** distinguish *a solution worked*, *an outcome moved*, and *the evidence supports causality*. These are different claims. Carry failed tests and unresolved risks into the strategy update, not just successes.

A harmful or misleading test is worse than a delayed test. If a broken user journey would make customers reject an otherwise good idea, fix the test before drawing a conclusion. For major launches, agree the scoreboard and guardrails before release and check the full product lifecycle afterwards, including support, maintenance, adoption and retirement.
