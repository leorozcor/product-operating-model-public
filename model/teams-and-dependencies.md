# Teams, decision boundaries and dependencies

*Serves: BELIEFS 2.3 (conditions for autonomy), 3.9 (cross-functional accountability), 4.9 (authority follows the value model), and the strategy-to-OKR loop.*

## The team is the unit of accountability

Product, Engineering and Design are peers in solving a customer and business problem, not departments passing work down a chain. The team owns the outcome together; relevant technical, design, commercial, data and operational expertise should influence the decision while it is still open. People can have different responsibilities without creating a hierarchy of whose evidence counts.

A team's mission and decision space must be explicit. Leadership sets strategic direction, constraints, investment boundaries and cross-team trade-offs; the team ordinarily chooses how to investigate and solve the problem inside those boundaries. A named accountable person makes a decision when a choice crosses the agreed boundary or is materially irreversible. A PM is not a substitute for the judgment of engineers, designers or domain specialists.

Before saying a team is empowered, ask: Does it have the authority, skills, context and access to users and data to be accountable for its outcome? If not, name the missing condition and its sponsor instead of calling the team autonomous.

## The extended team is real

Sales, Customer Support, Operations, Finance, Legal, Security, Research and Analytics can be essential to an outcome without being full-time members. Involve them during discovery where their knowledge changes the problem or constraints, rather than collecting approvals after a solution is chosen. Record actual regulatory or financial sign-offs; do not invent a universal approval chain.

For a platform or enabling team, ask which downstream team outcome its capability makes possible. Track operational health separately, and avoid measuring the platform only by tickets delivered to requesting teams.

## Before freezing term OKRs: a dependency handshake

`partners` in `krs.csv` signals a relationship; it does **not** prove the other team has agreed to do the work. For material dependencies, have the teams and relevant leader briefly agree:

1. Which outcome or KR needs help, and why the dependency matters.
2. What contribution or decision is needed, from whom, by when, and what evidence shows it is usable.
3. What each team is choosing not to do, or what must be reprioritised to make room.
4. Who can resolve a conflict that neither team can solve, and by when.

Use `templates/dependency-handshake.md` only when a dependency is consequential or repeatedly stalls. A short conversation and recorded agreement beat a standing committee. Dependencies discovered during the term belong in the gemba's blocker discussion and `actions.csv` when management action is required. Review repeated dependency failures as **system design problems**, not team defects.

## Questions for a term review

Did the team own an outcome or merely receive tasks? Did cross-functional input arrive before or after the decision? Did partners actually reserve capacity? Which dependency needed leadership intervention? What should change in team boundaries, incentives or decision rights next term?
